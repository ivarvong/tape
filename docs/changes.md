# Changes

What each release of the language changed, newest first, and the forms that went. [LANGUAGE.md](../LANGUAGE.md)
is the language as it is; this is how it came to be so, kept apart from it so that an agent learning the language
reads the definition once.

## Changes in 0.4

What 0.4 changed, from the 0.3 usability benchmark (48 agent runs) and its feedback, newest first. [What
went](#what-went), below, has the forms each replaced.

| 25 November 2026 | breaking: steps are a cost model, not only a termination bound (issue #19): step version 3. A built-in that reads or writes a number of bytes the program decides costs one step per 16 of them, rounded up, the price `scan` and `scan_plain` already had, and one that does a fixed amount of work costs nothing (LANGUAGE.md section 10). Newly priced: `append` (and so every text with holes, `++`, std's `write_text` and every body copied into an answer) and `parse_float`, which costs one step per 16 bytes, and one a byte and 64 for the call when the host reads the text (anything but a plain decimal of at most 2^53 in its digits and 22 after the point), and `write_float` (so a Float in a hole or in JSON), 128 for the host's call: those two charges are the cheapest call measured over an entry's 2.33 to 2.53 ns as a call that returns (write_float of 1/7 301 to 318 ns, 119 to 136 entries; parse_float's call 155 to 163 ns once its bytes are taken off, 61 to 70), rounded to a power of two. Measured on the bench suite (`node bench/suite/steps.ts`), 16 bytes take 0.34 ns to append, 0.48 to scan and 0.72 to scan_plain, within 2.1x of each other, while the host's read in parse_float takes 5.4 ns a byte, 250x an append's 16 bytes and about an entry's 1.0 to 2.4 ns. `len`, `slice`, `freeze`, `size`, `buf`, `push` and the other number built-ins stay free. Every count that copies text rises: hello's second request 91 to 96, the blog's cached GET 234 to 1,124 and its feed 223 to 1,117 (now 41 to 46 ns a step, from 196 to 231), md's render 63,515 to 65,482; csv and words move under 0.1%. |
